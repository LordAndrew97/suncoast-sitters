# Internal Data Handling Policy — DRAFT

> **DRAFT.** Review with your attorney. This one is for the two of you, not for
> publication. It is also the most important document in this folder.

Now that you publish almost nothing about your sitters, **the sensitive data you
hold privately is your largest legal exposure.** Social Security numbers,
background reports, driving records, reference contacts. If that leaks or sits
forever in a shared inbox, that is the incident that damages the business.

This is a short policy. Short is the point — a long one you do not follow is
worse than a short one you do.

---

## 1. Where sensitive records live

**One place, and only one place.** Choose it and write it here:

> Sensitive records are stored in: ______________________________

It must be: access-controlled, two-factor-authenticated, and shared only between
the two of you. A password manager with a shared vault, or a dedicated encrypted
drive folder, is fine. Free consumer Google Drive shared by link is not.

**Never store sensitive records in:** email inboxes, WhatsApp, phone camera rolls,
a laptop's Downloads folder, Slack, this Git repository, or anywhere on the
website. `.gitignore` blocks the obvious cases but it will not save you from a
determined mistake.

## 2. What counts as sensitive

- Social Security numbers
- Background check reports and driving records
- Copies of government ID
- Reference contact details and what references said
- Sitter home addresses and phone numbers
- Family home addresses, children's names and ages, and anything medical

Screening reports are **consumer report information** under federal law and carry
specific destruction obligations. Treat them as the most sensitive thing you hold.

## 3. Who may see what

| | Elena | Marcus | A family | A sitter |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Full background report | Yes | Yes | **Never** | Their own, on request |
| SSN, ID copies | Yes | Yes | **Never** | Their own |
| Sitter full name + photo | Yes | Yes | On confirmed booking, if the sitter consented | — |
| Reference contact details | Yes | Yes | Only those the sitter authorised | Their own |
| Family address, child details | Yes | Yes | — | Only their own bookings |

**A family never receives a background report.** Tell them the checks were
completed and what they covered. If they push, explain that handing over a
consumer report about a third party is not something you are permitted to do.

## 4. How long you keep things

| Record | Keep for | Then |
|---|---|---|
| Booking enquiries | [2] years | Delete |
| Background reports, driving records | [duration of listing + 2 years] | Destroy securely |
| Signed disclosures and authorizations | [duration of listing + 5 years] | Destroy securely |
| Adverse action correspondence | [5] years | Destroy securely |
| SSN and ID copies | Only as long as needed to complete screening | Destroy immediately after |
| Reference notes | Duration of listing | Destroy securely |

*[Attorney: set these periods. There are competing pressures — FCRA and the FTC
Disposal Rule push toward destroying early; defending a negligence claim pushes
toward keeping longer. This is exactly the trade-off to get advice on.]*

**Set a calendar reminder for the first working day of each quarter** to work
through retention. Nobody does this without a reminder.

## 5. Secure destruction

The FTC Disposal Rule requires reasonable measures to destroy consumer report
information so it cannot practicably be read or reconstructed.

- **Paper:** cross-cut shred. Not the recycling bin.
- **Digital:** delete from the store **and empty the trash**, including any cloud
  version history and any backup.
- **Never** put a background report in a household bin, and never forward one to
  a personal email address "to look at later".

## 6. If something goes wrong

If sensitive data is exposed — a lost laptop, a wrong-address email, a
compromised account — **contact your attorney the same day.** Florida has a data
breach notification statute with deadlines. Do not decide by yourselves that it
was probably fine.

Write down, immediately: what happened, when you found out, whose data, and what
you did. Contemporaneous notes matter enormously later.

## 7. Access hygiene

- Two-factor authentication on every account touching this business: email,
  storage, the screening provider, the domain registrar, GitHub, the form service.
- No shared passwords typed into a spreadsheet or a note. Use a password manager.
- If either of you leaves the business or a device is lost, rotate everything.
- Review who has access to what every six months.
