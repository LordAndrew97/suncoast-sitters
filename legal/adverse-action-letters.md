# Adverse Action Letters — DRAFT

> **DRAFT — NOT FOR USE.** Must be reviewed by a Florida attorney before use.

If you decide **not to list someone**, or to **remove someone**, wholly or partly
because of something in a background check, federal law requires a two-step
process. Skipping it is a straightforward statutory violation with damages and
attorney's fees attached, and it is easy to skip by accident because the natural
human instinct is simply to stop replying.

**Do not tell the person "the background check came back bad" on the phone and
leave it there.** Follow the two steps.

---

## Step 1 — Pre-adverse action notice

Send this **before** you make the decision final. Enclose:

1. A complete copy of the consumer report you relied on
2. A copy of the CFPB's "A Summary of Your Rights Under the Fair Credit
   Reporting Act"

Then **wait a reasonable period** before proceeding. Five business days is the
commonly used minimum. *[Attorney: confirm the period to use.]*

> **Subject: About your application to join [BRAND NAME]**
>
> Dear [Name],
>
> Thank you for applying to be listed with us.
>
> We are writing to let you know that we are considering not moving forward with
> your listing, and that our concern is based in part on information in a
> background report about you. We have not made a final decision yet.
>
> Enclosed you will find a complete copy of the report we received, and a summary
> of your rights under the Fair Credit Reporting Act.
>
> **The company that prepared the report did not make this decision and cannot
> explain it.** If you believe anything in the report is inaccurate or
> incomplete, you have the right to dispute it directly with them:
>
> [SCREENING COMPANY NAME], [address], [phone], [website]
>
> If you would like to tell us anything about the report, or give us context we
> do not have, please reply by **[DATE — at least five business days out]**. We
> will read it before deciding.
>
> [Your name], [BRAND NAME]

---

## Step 2 — Adverse action notice

Send this **only after** the waiting period, and only if you are still declining.

> **Subject: Your application to join [BRAND NAME]**
>
> Dear [Name],
>
> We have decided not to move forward with listing you with [BRAND NAME]. Our
> decision was based in whole or in part on information contained in a consumer
> report.
>
> The consumer reporting agency that provided the report is:
>
> [SCREENING COMPANY NAME], [address], [phone], [website]
>
> **That agency did not make this decision and is not able to explain the reasons
> for it.**
>
> You have the right to obtain a free copy of your consumer report from that
> agency if you request it within 60 days of receiving this notice. You also have
> the right to dispute the accuracy or completeness of any information in the
> report directly with the agency.
>
> A summary of your rights under the Fair Credit Reporting Act is enclosed.
>
> We appreciate the time you gave to your application.
>
> [Your name], [BRAND NAME]

---

## Keep records

For each person, keep: the signed disclosure and authorization, the report, both
letters, and the dates you sent them. Store them per `data-handling-policy.md`.

**Two things to be careful about.** First, if you decline someone for a reason
that has nothing to do with the report — they were unreliable, the interview went
badly, you simply have enough sitters — this process does not apply, but write
down the actual reason at the time. Reconstructing it later looks like a pretext.
Second, a criminal record is not automatically disqualifying, and blanket
exclusions can raise discrimination issues. Ask your attorney what a defensible
individualised assessment looks like for your business.
